\begin{abstract}
Grokking describes delayed generalization: a model fits its training set long before it reaches comparable performance on held-out data. We report three independently seeded PyTorch runs of a fixed one-layer Transformer configuration trained on modular addition, $(a+b)\bmod 113$, using a 30/70 train-validation split, full-batch AdamW, learning rate 0.001, weight decay 1.0, and 40{,}000 optimization steps. All three runs satisfy the same deterministic event policy and reach 100\% final validation accuracy. Memorization is detected at steps 200, 302, and 416 for seeds 42, 43, and 44 respectively; the first validation threshold crossing occurs at steps 25{,}600, 32{,}300, and 23{,}000; stable plateaus are detected at steps 26{,}500, 33{,}200, and 23{,}900. The timing of the transition varies substantially across seeds while the final outcome remains the same. Each run is backed by replayed checkpoints and artifact-integrity checks; the most recent seed-44 run additionally freezes source hashes, environment metadata, and exact dataset split indices. The contribution is a reproducibility-focused, evidence-first baseline for multi-seed delayed generalization rather than a new mechanistic theory of grokking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Grokking was introduced as a regime in which generalization emerges long after a model has already fit its training data \cite{power2022grokking}. Modular arithmetic provides a compact setting for studying this phenomenon under controlled conditions. Subsequent work has extended grokking beyond algorithmic data \cite{liu2022omnigrok}, examined it through mechanistic interpretability \cite{nanda2023progress}, and investigated representation and embedding dynamics \cite{alquboj2025embedding}.

This paper focuses on a narrower methodological question: can delayed generalization be reproduced across multiple random seeds under one fixed configuration while preserving enough evidence to audit the reported milestones?

We report three runs with seeds 42, 43, and 44. All use the same task, model family, optimizer, train split, and training budget. The runs differ in transition timing but converge to the same final validation accuracy. We further separate four distinct claims that are often conflated in experimental reporting: artifact integrity, checkpoint replay, executable training, and full long-run reproduction.

The project follows an evidence-first rule: measured quantities are generated by the training and verification pipeline; interpretation is downstream of those measurements.

\section{Related Work}
Power et al.\ \cite{power2022grokking} demonstrated delayed generalization on small algorithmic datasets, including modular arithmetic. Liu, Michaud, and Tegmark \cite{liu2022omnigrok} studied grokking through training and test loss geometry and showed that related behavior can appear beyond algorithmic data. Nanda et al.\ \cite{nanda2023progress} reverse-engineered small Transformers trained on modular addition and identified continuous mechanistic progress underlying the apparently abrupt transition. More recent work has examined the role of the embedding layer and related representational dynamics \cite{alquboj2025embedding}.

Our contribution is complementary. We do not propose a new causal mechanism. Instead, we package multi-seed grokking results with explicit milestone rules, replayed checkpoints, integrity checks, and documented provenance boundaries.

\section{Experimental Setup}
The task is modular addition:
\[
y=(a+b)\bmod 113.
\]
Each input is tokenized as \texttt{[a, b, EQUALS]} with context length 3. The model predicts the result at the last token position. The complete table contains $113^2=12{,}769$ ordered pairs and is partitioned into 30\% training and 70\% validation data.

\section{Milestone Detection Policy}
We use a deterministic event policy rather than selecting transition points post hoc.

Let $A_{\mathrm{train}}(t)$ and $A_{\mathrm{val}}(t)$ denote train and validation accuracy.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Memorization step:} first step with $A_{\mathrm{train}}\ge 0.98$.
    \item \textbf{Generalization candidate:} first checked step with $A_{\mathrm{val}}\ge 0.985$.
    \item \textbf{Stable plateau:} first point completing 10 consecutive generalization checks with $A_{\mathrm{val}}\ge 0.985$.
\end{itemize}

For the more recent runs, memorization is checked every optimizer step while generalization is checked every 100 optimizer steps. The seed-42 historical run logged memorization at 100-step resolution, so its value of 200 should be interpreted at that coarser resolution.

The repository label \texttt{GROKKING\_CONFIRMED} means that the stable-plateau rule is satisfied under this policy; it is not intended as a universal scientific definition of grokking.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Three-seed replication}
All three principal runs reach 1.0 final train accuracy and 1.0 final validation accuracy. Their transition times differ markedly.

The candidate transition spans a range of 9{,}300 optimizer steps across the three seeds, from 23{,}000 to 32{,}300. Stable plateaus span the same 9{,}300-step range, from 23{,}900 to 33{,}200. Thus, under the fixed configuration used here, the final outcome is replicated but the timing is not seed-invariant.

The delay from detected memorization to the first validation threshold crossing is 25{,}400 steps for seed 42, 31{,}998 for seed 43, and 22{,}584 for seed 44. This variation is itself a useful experimental result: a single transition step should not be treated as a configuration-level constant.

\subsection{Seed 42 reference run}
The original frozen run contains 401 records from step 0 through step 40{,}000. At the memorization checkpoint (step 200), replayed train accuracy is approximately 0.9984 while validation accuracy is approximately 0.00045. At step 25{,}600 validation accuracy reaches approximately 0.9978, and by step 26{,}500 it reaches 1.0.

\subsection{Seed 43 replication}
Under the same principal configuration, seed 43 memorizes at step 302, first crosses the 0.985 validation threshold at step 32{,}300, and reaches the stable plateau at step 33{,}200. The final train and validation accuracy are both 1.0. Checkpoint replay reports exact agreement with recorded metrics.

\subsection{Seed 44 replication and strengthened provenance}
Seed 44 memorizes at step 416, crosses the validation threshold at step 23{,}000, and reaches the stable plateau at step 23{,}900. The final train and validation accuracy are both 1.0.

This run was executed under a strengthened publication protocol. In addition to ordinary checkpoint and timeseries evidence, it records:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item an explicit \texttt{dataset\_split.json} containing the exact train/validation indices;
    \item a source-hash manifest using SHA-256;
    \item an environment lock containing Python, PyTorch, NumPy, platform, device, and dtype;
    \item checkpoint metadata binding each checkpoint to configuration, split, source, and environment hashes.
\end{itemize}

The replay audit reports \texttt{PASS} for checkpoint hashes, checkpoint inventory, temporal consistency, source freeze, environment, dataset split, architecture contract, and tokenization round-trip checks, with a maximum replay metric difference of 0.0.

\section{Negative and Boundary Controls}
The project also contains small-modulus runs used as negative or boundary controls. On $p=11$, tested configurations reached memorization or partial generalization without satisfying the grokking criterion. For example, configurations with $d_{\mathrm{model}}\in\{32,64\}$ and $d_{\mathrm{mlp}}\in\{128,256\}$ produced final validation accuracies ranging from roughly 3.5\% to 20\%, depending on seed and capacity, while remaining below the 0.985 grokking threshold.

These controls matter because the pipeline is not designed to label every successful training run as grokking. The event classifier can return memorization-only or incomplete-generalization outcomes.

\section{Reproducibility and Provenance}
The release separates four levels of evidence.

\paragraph{Artifact integrity.}
Published artifacts are hashed and checked for expected structure, finite values, and temporal consistency.

\paragraph{Checkpoint replay.}
Selected PyTorch state-dict checkpoints are loaded into the recorded architecture and evaluated on reconstructed or explicitly stored dataset splits. Replay metrics are compared against the frozen reports.

\paragraph{Executable training path.}
The public package contains a real PyTorch training path with forward pass, backward pass, AdamW update, and artifact generation.

\paragraph{Long-run replication.}
The three runs reported here are independent long runs differing by seed. They are not averaged replicas of one stored trajectory.

The strength of provenance is not identical across the three historical runs. Seed 44 was produced after the publication protocol was strengthened with explicit source, split, and environment hashing. Earlier runs remain replay-verified, but do not preserve every provenance field introduced later.

\section{Limitations}
This study supports a narrow claim: under one fixed modular-addition configuration, delayed generalization was reproduced in three independently seeded runs.

It does not establish:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item universality across arbitrary seeds;
    \item robustness across moduli, architectures, optimizers, regularization values, or train fractions;
    \item transfer to language or multimodal tasks;
    \item a causal mechanism for the transition;
    \item bitwise-identical retraining across hardware and software environments.
\end{itemize}

The number of seeds is still small. Three successful seeds support replication under this configuration, but they are insufficient for a strong distributional claim about seed robustness. In addition, provenance quality improved over the lifetime of the project: the seed-44 run includes exact source, split, and environment hashes that were not all preserved in the earliest historical run.

Finally, checksums are integrity controls for the published package; they are not third-party attestation.

\section{Discussion}
The central empirical observation is twofold. First, delayed generalization repeats across three independent seeds under the same principal configuration. Second, the transition time varies substantially across seeds.

This suggests that reporting only one grokking step can be misleading. A more informative protocol should report a distribution of memorization, candidate, and plateau steps across seeds, along with the exact event policy and logging resolution.

The provenance results lead to a second methodological point: reproducibility is not one binary property. Artifact hashes, checkpoint replay, source freezing, split preservation, environment capture, and full reruns answer different questions. Treating them as separate evidence layers makes the scientific boundary of a result easier to audit.

A natural next step is a larger seed sweep under the same frozen protocol, followed by controlled interventions on weight decay, train fraction, and architecture while preserving identical evidence gates.

\section{Conclusion}
We report reproducible delayed generalization across three independently seeded Transformer runs on modular addition modulo 113. All three runs reach perfect final validation accuracy, but their first generalization-threshold crossings occur at substantially different times: 23{,}000, 25{,}600, and 32{,}300 steps.

The result supports replication under the tested configuration while simultaneously showing that transition timing is seed-sensitive. The associated Grokking Lab package emphasizes auditable evidence through deterministic event rules, replayed checkpoints, artifact integrity checks, and progressively stronger provenance capture.
